Grass pollen is a major trigger for sneezing, itchy eyes and stuffy noses once the weather warms up. In New Jersey, that usually means late spring and early summer, when lawns, parks, athletic fields and roadsides are growing fast. This guide covers the timing, the symptoms, what helps, and a lesser-known problem: reactions to certain raw fruits and vegetables.

## When is grass pollen season in New Jersey?

Grass pollen season in New Jersey generally runs from late spring into early summer. The exact start and end change from year to year with temperature and rainfall, so a fixed calendar date is not reliable.

Grass season also overlaps with the tail end of tree season. A 2016 review of pollen allergens noted that grasses, trees and weeds often share habitats and flowering periods. That makes it hard to tell from symptoms alone which one is the culprit. Allergy testing can sort this out.

Weather matters day to day. Dry, warm, breezy days tend to spread more pollen. Rain can wash some out of the air for a short time. Lawn mowing can also stir up pollen right at nose level.

## What are the symptoms of a grass pollen allergy?

The usual symptoms are sneezing, a runny or blocked nose, itchy and watery eyes, and an itchy throat or roof of the mouth. Some people also cough or wheeze, especially if they have asthma.

The table below helps separate grass allergy from a cold.

| Clue | Grass pollen allergy | Common cold |
|---|---|---|
| Itchy eyes and nose | Common | Uncommon |
| Fever or body aches | No | Possible |
| Mucus | Usually clear and thin | Can turn thick or colored |
| How long it lasts | Weeks, as long as pollen is around | Usually about a week or so |
| Pattern | Worse outdoors, on dry windy days, after mowing | Not tied to location |

A review of pollen allergy also noted that up to 30% of people in industrialized countries have pollen-related type I allergies. Hay fever is common, and it is not "just in your head" or a sign of weakness.

## How can you reduce grass pollen exposure at home?

You cannot avoid grass pollen entirely, but a few habits cut how much comes inside. Most of these are common, well-established advice.

- **Shower and wash your hair after being outside.** Pollen collects on hair and skin. Washing at night keeps it off your pillow.
- **Change clothes when you come in.** Put outdoor clothes in the hamper.
- **Keep windows closed on dry, windy days.** Use air conditioning instead, and keep the filter clean.
- **Let someone else mow.** If you must do it, wear a snug mask, and shower after.
- **Wipe pets after walks.** Dogs and cats carry pollen in their fur.
- **Dry laundry indoors during peak days.** Sheets on an outdoor line pick up pollen.
- **Plan outdoor exercise around the weather.** Many people feel better after a rain than on a dry, breezy afternoon.

In New Jersey suburbs, lawns are often right outside the door. At the shore, dune and beach grasses grow close to where people spend summer days. Both can add to exposure, so these habits apply in both places.

## What medicines help with grass pollen allergy?

Over-the-counter options include oral antihistamines, nasal steroid sprays, antihistamine eye drops and saline rinses. They relieve symptoms rather than remove the allergy. Nasal steroid sprays tend to work best when started a week or two before symptoms usually begin and used daily. A pharmacist or doctor can help match a product to your symptoms.

Nasal dryness is a common side effect of the season. Antihistamine pills, decongestant sprays and cold indoor air conditioning can dry out the nose. A dry nose can feel crusty and irritated, which adds to the misery. A nasal moisturizer such as Narisure soothes and moisturizes dry nasal passages and can be used alongside your allergy plan. ## Can allergy tablets or shots help with grass pollen?

Yes, for some people. Allergen immunotherapy gradually trains the immune system to tolerate an allergen. For grass pollen, it comes as under-the-tongue tablets or as allergy shots.

A 2024 review described what is known about the tablets:

- Tablet immunotherapy for grass pollen rhinitis was first approved about 15 years ago for adults and for children over 5.
- Side effects are mostly local, in the mouth, and usually mild to moderate. They often go away on their own.
- A literature search found nine reported cases of anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction. No deaths were reported.
- After the first anaphylaxis report, manufacturers advised giving the first dose in a medical facility, with observation for at least 30 minutes.
- The authors noted no anaphylaxis reports from tablets in recent years.

The limits matter. The review gathered published case reports, so it cannot give an exact risk rate. It also covers tablets only, not shots. Immunotherapy is a long commitment, usually lasting years, and it is a decision to make with an allergist. Treatment usually needs to start well before the season, not in the middle of it.

Testing can also help. A 2016 review explained that newer molecular tests can tell true sensitization from cross-reactivity that often has no real-world effect. That matters when someone is allergic to several pollens and is considering immunotherapy.

## Why does grass allergy make some raw fruits and vegetables itch?

Some proteins in raw plant foods look similar to proteins in pollen. The immune system can mistake one for the other. This is called oral allergy syndrome, or pollen-food allergy syndrome.

A 2018 review of oral allergy syndrome reported these points:

- Symptoms are mostly itching of the mouth and throat that starts quickly after eating the food and fades within a couple of minutes after swallowing.
- People with grass allergy may react to peaches, oranges, celery, tomatoes and melons.
- Frequency among people with pollen allergy was reported at 5-8%. Of those, 1-2% had severe reactions such as anaphylaxis.
- Raw foods trigger reactions, while the same food cooked often does not.

A separate 2025 review agreed that symptoms are usually limited to the mouth but can sometimes involve the nose, lungs, skin or, rarely, anaphylaxis. It added that no treatment currently lets people safely eat the problem raw foods, and that avoiding them is the basis of prevention. Immunotherapy for this condition has not been established.

A 2018 review of a protein called profilin adds more detail. Profilin is found in all pollens and plant foods. It is linked with allergy to grass pollen and to melon-family fruits such as cucumber and squash. The review noted that profilin sensitization can complicate diagnosis and the choice of immunotherapy. These are reviews, not trials, so they describe patterns rather than prove who will react to what.

If a food makes your mouth itch during grass season, tell your doctor. Do not assume it is harmless if you also have throat tightness, trouble breathing, hives or swelling.

## When should you see a doctor about grass pollen allergy?

See a doctor if over-the-counter medicines are not enough, or if symptoms keep you from sleeping, working or going to school. Allergy testing can confirm grass as the trigger and open the door to immunotherapy.

Get care promptly for any of these:

- Wheezing, chest tightness or shortness of breath
- Mouth, lip or throat swelling after eating a raw fruit or vegetable
- Facial pain and thick colored mucus for more than 10 days, or fever
- Symptoms that last well past the grass season
- Frequent nosebleeds from a dry, irritated nose

Call 911 for trouble breathing, throat swelling, dizziness or fainting after eating or an allergen exposure.
