A crusty nose is common, and it is usually a sign of dry nasal lining, not a serious problem. The crusts are mostly dried mucus, sometimes mixed with dried blood. Picking them off is the habit that tends to make things worse, so a gentler routine matters.

## Why does my nose get crusty?

**Nasal crusts form when mucus dries out faster than the nose can replace it.** A healthy nose is lined with a thin, wet layer of mucus that traps dust and germs. When that layer dries, it hardens into flakes or scabs that stick to the lining.

Three things drive most cases:

- **Dry air.** Winter heating, air conditioning, airplanes and desert climates pull moisture from the lining.
- **Irritation or small damage.** Frequent nose blowing, picking, or a small scratch can leave a tiny scab that builds up crust.
- **Medicines and devices.** Some nasal sprays, allergy pills and oxygen or airway equipment can dry the nose.

A crust is also a natural bandage. When the lining is scraped, blood and fluid dry over the spot. Pulling it off removes that cover and often causes a new bleed.

## What are the common causes of nasal crusting?

**Dryness and minor irritation cause most crusts, but a few other causes are worth knowing.** The table shows how they compare.

| Cause | How common | Typical clues |
|---|---|---|
| Dry indoor or outdoor air | Very common | Crusts worse in winter or after travel; both nostrils |
| Picking or frequent wiping | Very common | Crusts on one side, small spots of blood |
| Allergy or cold with lots of blowing | Common | Sore, red skin at the nostril opening |
| Nasal sprays or oxygen use | Common | Crusting starts after beginning a product or device |
| After nose or sinus surgery | Expected in some people | Crusting during the weeks after the operation |
| Hole in the septum (perforation) | Uncommon | Crusting, nosebleeds, whistling when breathing |
| Fungal infection (mucormycosis) | Very rare | Grayish-black crusts, pain, swelling, fever |

If the crusts come with several of the last three clues, the cause deserves a closer look. The sections below explain why.

## How can I clear nasal crusts safely?

**Soften them first, then let them loosen on their own or wipe them away gently.** Hard pulling is what causes damage.

### A simple routine

1. **Wash your hands.** Dirty fingers carry germs into a scratched nose.
2. **Soften the crust.** Use a saline spray or gel for 2 to 3 sprays per nostril. A warm shower or breathing steam from a bowl of warm water for a few minutes also helps.
3. **Wait 5 to 10 minutes.** The crust should loosen and feel less stuck.
4. **Blow gently.** Blow one nostril at a time, with light pressure.
5. **Wipe only what comes away easily.** A damp cotton swab or tissue just inside the nostril is enough. Do not push it deep.
6. **Moisturize afterward.** A thin layer of a nasal moisturizer helps keep the lining from drying again. An oil-based option such as Narisure is a nasal moisturizer that soothes and moisturizes dry nasal passages. Saline sprays and water-based gels are other options.

If you use a saline rinse, distilled, sterile or previously boiled and cooled water is the safe choice. The FDA advises against using plain tap water for sinus rinsing because of rare but serious infections. ## What should I avoid doing?

**Avoid anything that tears the lining or dries it more.** Quick fixes often cause a longer problem.

- **Do not pick or dig with fingernails.** This is the top cause of repeated crusts and bleeds.
- **Do not use cotton swabs deep in the nose.** Keep them at the opening.
- **Do not use petroleum jelly in large amounts or push it far inside.** A tiny amount at the nostril edge is the limit, because oil that is inhaled can irritate the lungs.
- **Do not overuse decongestant sprays.** They dry the lining and can cause rebound stuffiness.
- **Do not use tap water for rinses.**

If picking starts a bleed, lean forward, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 full minutes and breathe through the mouth.

## Can crusting be a sign of a hole in the septum?

**Yes. Persistent crusting, especially with nosebleeds or a whistling sound, can be a sign of a septal perforation.** The septum is the wall of cartilage and lining between the two nostrils. A hole in it changes airflow and dries the edges, which makes crusts and bleeding more likely.

A 2022 review in *Facial Plastic Surgery* (Kimura and colleagues, Vanderbilt) notes that some people with a perforation have no symptoms, while others have nasal obstruction, crusting, nosebleeds and whistling. The review says management depends on the symptoms and the features of the hole. Some can be managed with conservative or minimally invasive steps, while others need surgery. It also notes there is no standardized repair method.

A 2022 report in the *Annals of Otology, Rhinology & Laryngology* (Bansberg and colleagues, Mayo Clinic Arizona) gives a sense of who is affected. It was a retrospective record review of 44 patients, 31 of them female, with an average age of 53.3 years. The most common symptoms were nasal obstruction, crusting and nosebleeds. The most common cause was previous septal surgery, at 45.5%. The average hole was about 11.8 mm long. Among 43 patients with follow-up, 41 (95.3%) had complete closure after surgery. Four patients needed a second operation for ongoing blockage.

The limits matter. This was one center, it looked back at records, and it had no comparison group. It describes people already referred for surgery, so it cannot say how common perforations are among everyone with crusty noses. Most crusty noses do not have a hole. A doctor can check with a quick look inside the nose.

## Is crusting normal after sinus surgery?

**Some crusting after nose or sinus surgery is expected, and surgeons usually give specific cleaning instructions.** Follow those first.

A 2023 study in *The Laryngoscope* (Seresirikachorn and colleagues) followed 45 adults who had frontal sinus surgery using a technique called Carolyn's window. Early after surgery (within 90 days), crusting was reported in 2.4% of patients, adhesions in 4.8%, pain in 1.2% and bleeding in 1.2%. The study was a case series from one group and did not compare techniques. The numbers show that crusting can happen after surgery, but they do not predict any one person's experience.

## When is nasal crusting an emergency?

**Grayish-black crusts with pain, facial swelling, fever or vision changes need urgent care.** This pattern can signal sinonasal mucormycosis, a rare and dangerous fungal infection. It occurs mostly in people with diabetes or weakened immune systems.

A 2019 report in the *Indian Journal of Otolaryngology* (Singh and colleagues) described 26 patients treated at one hospital in India between 2013 and 2017. Doctors suspected the infection based on grayish-black crusting seen on nasal endoscopy, then confirmed it with tissue testing. All patients received surgical debridement (removal of damaged tissue), correction of the underlying immune problem and intravenous antifungal medicine. Only 10 of the 26 had a successful outcome. The authors stress early detection. This was a small hospital series, so it shows how serious the disease can be, not how common it is. Everyday dry crusts are almost never this.

## How can I keep crusts from coming back?

**Keep the lining moist and stop the cycle of irritation.**

- Use a humidifier in the bedroom and aim for roughly 30% to 50% indoor humidity. Clean it as the maker directs.
- Use saline spray or a gentle moisturizer once or twice a day during dry months.
- Drink water through the day.
- Trim fingernails short, and blow gently.
- Ask a pharmacist whether any medicine you take dries the nose.

## When should I see a doctor?

See a doctor if crusting lasts more than 2 to 3 weeks despite moisturizing, or if you notice any of these:

- Nosebleeds that keep returning or do not stop after 20 minutes of firm pressure
- A whistling sound when breathing through the nose
- Crusts on one side only, with ongoing blockage
- Pus, a bad smell or fever
- Facial pain, swelling or vision changes
- Gray or black crusts, especially with diabetes or a weak immune system (seek care the same day)
- Crusting after a nose operation that is getting worse
